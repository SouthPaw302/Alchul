# Canon

## Locked foundations

- The founding population is terrestrial Earth life.
- It descends from a Late Cretaceous Appalachian maniraptoran / dromaeosaur-grade theropod population associated with the northern Mississippi Embayment / future New Madrid region.
- The exact founder taxon is not currently given a modern scientific species name in canon.
- The founders are feathered, predatory, grasping-forelimbed theropods with strong visual intelligence and flexible behavior.
- Their social behavior is not assumed to be wolf-pack-like. The working baseline is facultative sociality: solitary behavior, pair bonds, kin tolerance, juvenile learning, temporary aggregation, and opportunistic cooperation.
- Fermentation and recurring access to altered states become important in the early divergence of the New Madrid population.
- Alchul is real within the story, but Alchul's true nature, origin, sex/gender, body, and ontology remain unresolved.
- Alchul influences development gradually over immense spans of time. The civilization does not arise in a single leap.
- Religion and science emerge together rather than as cleanly separate institutions.
- The population eventually descends into a vast natural subterranean biosphere before the K–Pg catastrophe.
- The deep world is natural, luminous in places, ecologically rich, and independently shaped by geological and biological processes.
- Some lineages are born entirely underground.
- The civilization survives the K–Pg surface catastrophe.
- Over the following ~66 million years, the descendants diverge biologically and culturally, eventually becoming a civilization far more advanced than modern humanity by its own nonhuman developmental path.
- Their technology evolves from their own biology, cognition, ecology, geophysics, chemistry, collective states, and access to aspects of reality humans cannot normally perceive.
- Modern New Madrid seismic activity is connected in-universe to their deep civilization and infrastructure.
- Human myths about reptilian or serpent beings may contain distorted echoes of encounters, but no specific historical myth is automatically treated as literal evidence.
