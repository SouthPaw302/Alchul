# Founder Research — Late Cretaceous New Madrid / Mississippi Embayment

Status: active research notes. This file is not canon by itself.

## Geographic and geological setting

The future New Madrid region lay near the northern Mississippi Embayment during the Late Cretaceous. The area shifted between nonmarine coastal-plain, deltaic, riverine, lacustrine, and shallow nearshore environments as sea level and sedimentation changed.

Useful formations and regional anchors:
- Post Creek Formation — Campanian nonmarine coastal-plain deposits in southeastern Missouri / southern Illinois.
- McNairy Sand — Maastrichtian nonmarine to nearshore deposits beneath the New Madrid region.
- Chronister site, southeastern Missouri — Late Cretaceous terrestrial/lacustrine assemblage including `Parrosaurus missouriensis` and fragmentary theropod material.
- Coon Creek Formation, western Tennessee — Campanian shallow marine/coastal setting useful for reconstructing nearby Appalachian coastal ecology.

## Founder lineage

Best current working choice:
- unnamed Appalachian dromaeosaurid / maniraptoran population
- small-to-medium body size; do not lock exact mass yet
- predatory or opportunistically omnivorous ecology
- feathered; feathering is strongly supported across Dromaeosauridae
- digitigrade hindlimbs
- long balancing tail
- enlarged second-toe claw
- three-fingered grasping forelimbs
- toothed predatory skull
- binocular vision
- relatively sophisticated brain for a non-avian dinosaur
- elevated metabolism / endothermic or heterothermic physiology is plausible for derived maniraptorans

The exact species should remain unresolved until research justifies a narrower assignment.

## Feathering

Direct feather preservation and/or quill knobs occur across major dromaeosaurid clades, including `Velociraptor`, `Dineobellator`, `Microraptor`, `Zhenyuanlong`, and others. The safest reconstruction for the founder population is therefore a substantially feathered animal, not a scaly Jurassic-Park style raptor.

Working reconstruction:
- pennaceous feathers on the forelimbs
- body plumage over most of the torso
- feathered tail likely
- scaled lower legs and feet
- exposed facial integument possible around the snout and eyes

## Hands and wrists

The founder forelimb is not a primate hand but is already unusually promising for later manipulation:
- three functional digits
- curved manual claws
- mobile maniraptoran wrist with semilunate carpal mechanics
- capacity to seize, hook, brace, pull, hold prey, and manipulate environmental objects

Do not depict a modern opposable thumb at founder stage.

## Reproduction and juvenile development

Related maniraptorans show a mosaic of reptile-like and bird-like reproductive traits.

Troodontid evidence supports:
- iterative egg laying
- two functional reproductive tracts in at least some forms
- partly exposed/open nest architecture
- brooding or nest attendance
- incubation periods intermediate between modern reptiles and birds
- relatively high parental investment compared with many more basal dinosaurs
- precocial young were possible

A 2023 isotopic study of `Troodon` also supports heterothermic endothermy and suggests communal nesting by multiple females in at least that lineage.

For Alchul canon this means prolonged nest attendance, repeated use of nesting sites, juvenile observation of adults, and social learning are all biologically reasonable before civilization.

## Social behavior

Do not write stable wolf-like pack hunting as fact.

Stable-isotope work on `Deinonychus` found different juvenile and adult diets and interpreted that pattern as evidence against mammal-style cooperative packs.

Safer working model:
- individuals can hunt alone
- pair or kin tolerance
- juvenile learning
- temporary aggregation around food, reproduction, water, nesting, or environmental opportunities
- communal nesting possible in related maniraptoran lineages
- opportunistic cooperation possible
- social behavior likely variable and context-dependent

This model is especially useful because the Harvest can become one of the pressures increasing sustained congregation.

## Ecological role

The founders are mesopredators rather than dominant megafaunal predators.

Likely neighboring groups in broader Appalachia:
- hadrosaurs
- tyrannosauroids
- ornithomimosaurs
- nodosaurids / ankylosaurs
- crocodilians
- turtles
- fishes
- marine reptiles and sharks in nearby coastal/marine settings

## Plants, fruit, and fermentation

Eastern North American Cretaceous floras preserve abundant angiosperm flowers, fruits, seeds, and stamens. Campanian-age floras from Georgia show that flowering plants were already diverse in eastern North America. Large angiosperm trees existed in North America by the Turonian.

The exact fruit species at New Madrid is unknown, so canon should not name a modern fruit.

What is scientifically safe:
- fruiting angiosperms existed
- sugary plant tissues created microbial niches
- yeast lineages associated with ethanol production were diversifying during the Cretaceous
- evolutionary work on yeast links the rise of aerobic alcoholic fermentation to the spread of sugar-rich fruits

Therefore naturally fermented plant material is a plausible ecological trigger, but the exact chemistry of the founder Harvest remains an open research question.

## Why this lineage works narratively and biologically

A mesopredator benefits from:
- memory
- visual attention
- prediction
- stealth
- opportunism
- rapid learning
- behavioral flexibility
- manipulation with grasping forelimbs
- repeated use of successful sites
- observation of older individuals

Those traits provide a plausible biological platform for later cultural and technical development without requiring a sudden intelligence jump.

## Burial / fossil-record implication

The local terrestrial Cretaceous record is poor and heavily buried beneath younger Mississippi Embayment sediments. A lineage living there could be real within the story without leaving an obvious, easily exposed diagnostic fossil record.

Later cultural treatment of dead individuals may further reduce fossil visibility.

## Research questions still open

- founder body mass and total length
- exact likely prey spectrum
- detailed brain/endocast comparison with troodontids and eudromaeosaurs
- limits of wrist rotation and object handling
- exact feather distribution for an Appalachian form

- chemical signaling / chemosensory plausibility
- local Campanian-Maastrichtian plant taxa and fruit types
- fungi, yeasts, and other psychoactive/toxic compounds in the region
- local climate and seasonality
- realistic routes into shallow subsurface environments

## Research anchors

Key sources used in this pass:
- Turner et al. / AMNH summary: quill knobs in `Velociraptor`, confirming feathers.
- Jasinski et al. 2020, Scientific Reports: `Dineobellator` and widespread feather evidence across Dromaeosauridae.
- Varricchio et al. 1997, Nature: troodontid nests, paired eggs, partial burial, brooding.
- Varricchio et al. 2018, Scientific Reports: intermediate incubation and primitive brooding in `Troodon`.
- Tagliavento et al. 2023, PNAS: `Troodon` heterothermy, reptile-like eggshell mineralization, possible shared nests.
- Frederickson et al. 2020, Palaeogeography Palaeoclimatology Palaeoecology: juvenile/adult `Deinonychus` diet differences and evidence against mammal-style pack hunting.
- Crane & Herendeen 1996: eastern North American Cretaceous flowers and fruits.
- Hagman et al. 2014 and Thomson et al. 2005: Cretaceous evolution of yeast alcoholic fermentation associated with fruit-rich niches.
