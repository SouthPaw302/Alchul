# Founder Research — Late Cretaceous New Madrid / Mississippi Embayment

Status: active research notes. This file is not canon by itself.

## Geographic and geological setting

The future New Madrid region lay near the northern Mississippi Embayment during the Late Cretaceous. The area shifted between nonmarine coastal-plain, deltaic, riverine, lacustrine, and shallow nearshore environments as sea level and sedimentation changed.

Useful formations and regional anchors:
- Post Creek Formation — Campanian nonmarine coastal-plain deposits in southeastern Missouri / southern Illinois.
- McNairy Sand — Maastrichtian nonmarine to nearshore deposits beneath the New Madrid region.
- Chronister site, southeastern Missouri — Late Cretaceous terrestrial/lacustrine assemblage including `Parrosaurus missouriensis` and fragmentary theropod material.
- Coon Creek Formation, western Tennessee — Campanian shallow marine/coastal setting useful for reconstructing nearby Appalachian coastal ecology.

## Founder lineage

Best current working choice:
- unnamed Appalachian dromaeosaurid / maniraptoran population
- small-to-medium body size
- predatory or opportunistically omnivorous ecology
- feathered
- digitigrade hindlimbs
- long balancing tail
- enlarged second-toe claw
- three-fingered grasping forelimbs
- toothed predatory skull
- binocular vision
- relatively sophisticated brain for a non-avian dinosaur
- high activity/metabolic capacity

The exact species should remain unresolved until research justifies a narrower assignment.

## Social behavior

Do not write stable wolf-like pack hunting as fact.

Safer working model:
- individuals can hunt alone
- pair or kin tolerance
- juvenile learning
- temporary aggregation around food, reproduction, or environmental opportunities
- opportunistic cooperation possible
- social behavior likely variable and context-dependent

This model is especially useful because the Harvest can become one of the pressures increasing sustained congregation.

## Ecological role

The founders are mesopredators rather than dominant megafaunal predators.

Likely neighboring groups in broader Appalachia:
- hadrosaurs
- tyrannosauroids
- ornithomimosaurs
- nodosaurids / ankylosaurs
- crocodilians
- turtles
- fishes
- marine reptiles and sharks in nearby coastal/marine settings

## Why this lineage works narratively and biologically

A mesopredator benefits from:
- memory
- visual attention
- prediction
- stealth
- opportunism
- rapid learning
- behavioral flexibility
- manipulation with grasping forelimbs

Those traits provide a plausible biological platform for later cultural and technical development without requiring a sudden intelligence jump.

## Burial / fossil-record implication

The local terrestrial Cretaceous record is poor and heavily buried beneath younger Mississippi Embayment sediments. A lineage living there could be real within the story without leaving an obvious, easily exposed diagnostic fossil record.

Later cultural treatment of dead individuals may further reduce fossil visibility.
